WarStrike Game Controller

Consulting · Manufacturable iOS game controller designed for mass production

A consulting project to design and deliver a production-ready physical game controller for WarStrike — a first-person shooter iOS game. The controller is a gun-shaped enclosure that mounts an iPhone on top, with a physical trigger that communicates with the app over Bluetooth. I took the project from concept through prototyping, full BOM, assembly documentation, and a China manufacturing pipeline ready for high-volume production.

Electronics & Firmware

  • ESP32-CAM Module: Core microcontroller handling Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) communication with the WarStrike iOS app. Each unit is flashed with a unique GUN_ID via Arduino IDE so multiple controllers can pair simultaneously.
  • Micro Limit Switch Trigger: Physical trigger actuates a micro limit switch with a 10ms debounce delay — fast enough to register single shots reliably without false inputs.
  • 2600mAh Battery Pack: Rechargeable via USB-C, housed in the grip with a sliding battery latch for easy swapping.
  • BLE Communication: Controller advertises its own BLE service UUID; the app pairs on connection and receives shot-fired events in real time.

Mechanical Design

  • 6-Part Enclosure: Split-shell design — Grip Right, Grip Left (combined into a single part for production), Barrel Right, Barrel Left (combined), Trigger, and Battery Latch. Designed for ABS or PA injection molding at scale.
  • Brass Threaded Inserts: 8× heat-set brass inserts for durable, reusable fastening with M3 and M2.5 screws — no glue, fully serviceable.
  • Phone Mount: ¼" screw-mount phone clamp on the barrel rail, compatible with all iPhone sizes. Holds the screen in the shooter's line of sight.
  • Prototyped in 3D Print: Multiple iterations in PLA across green and blue colorways before finalizing the production geometry.

Manufacturing & Supply Chain

  • China Supplier Sourcing: Identified and qualified high-volume manufacturing partners in China for injection-molded plastic enclosures, covering tooling, lead times, and per-unit cost targets.
  • Full BOM: Delivered a complete bill of materials covering all internal electronics, hardware, and plastic parts with supplier links, part counts, and cost breakdowns ready for procurement.
  • Assembly Documentation: Produced step-by-step assembly instructions and ESP32 flashing guide so production staff can build and program units without engineering involvement.
  • Scalable Firmware Pipeline: Flashing workflow designed for throughput — each unit gets a unique Gun ID incremented per flash, using Arduino IDE with a simple compile-and-flash cycle.
